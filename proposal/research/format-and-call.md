**Synthesis.** Chapter-level sources consistently ask for a sharp focus, credible method, and explicit fit. The stronger public examples make a compact argument before outlining contents. Book-level sources add the useful tests of audience, distinctiveness, and coherent chapter trajectory. For this IGI call, the proposed narrative therefore moves from the attribution problem to the matched comparison, evidence and limits, then closes with the value to mobile-security readers. A long administrative outline or a mini review would obscure the central research question.

## Repository evidence and novelty boundary

Reviewed `README.md`, `CITATION.cff`, `configs/data.yaml`, `configs/experiments.yaml`, `docs/Roadmap.md` (scope, protocol, novelty and limitations), `docs/Research Evidence.md` (especially sections 28–35 and collision audit), `docs/results.md`, `docs/Audit Matrix.md`, implementation under `src/ctk_android/`, and result/extension artifact inventory. The project's scientific object is a **binary Android malware detector** scored on AVClass2 family-labelled positives; family identity is used to construct exposure contrasts, not as the model's prediction target. Four market/era domains are simulated clients from one LAMDA/AndroZoo corpus. Its main comparison holds the target client's data fixed and contrasts local training, collaborative training with the family absent everywhere, and collaborative training with that family present among peers while controlling training volume. The measured quantity is recall on the locally absent family at client-calibrated benign false-positive targets. The chapter can examine federation-wide versus own-domain and worst-client outcomes, exact peer dose, wrong-family placebo, family heterogeneity, and full-exposure reference.

Literature stress test: [CELEST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11459) shows local versus federated benefit on a family held by another network; [CyberForce](https://doi.org/10.1109/TDSC.2025.3547005) contains an **unmatched** absent-everywhere endpoint and peer-holder sweep for IoT malware mitigation; [FLEKD-IDS](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICC51166.2024.10622262), published at IEEE ICC 2024 (pp. 2034–2039), drops attack classes at clients and reports per-class detection. Thus neither transfer from peers nor a missing-class test is claimed as new. The defensible distinction is the *volume-matched attribution contrast* and its Android, own-domain and worst-client interpretation. This is a bounded finding in accessible literature, not a priority claim. `docs/Research Evidence.md` reports that FID-SPA and FEDroid full texts were unavailable; the pre-submission author should recheck them and forward citations.

Second novelty search on 30 September 2026: the [January 2026 Android FL study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950584925002319) and [M2FD](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167404825000501) address federated Android detection and non-IID/drift, but the accessible descriptions do not report the paired family-exposure decomposition. [FID-SPA](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11627910/) directly targets incomplete attack-category sets in IoT intrusion detection; its abstract presents semantic sharing, while full-text contrasts remain unverified. [Droidware](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0952197626019226) addresses secure, robust Android FL; no matching attribution contrast appears in its accessible description. These records narrow the novelty claim to a specific evaluation design, not a general claim about FL, rare threats, or class transfer.
